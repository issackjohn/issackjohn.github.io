I Got ChatGPT to Monitor the Visa Bulletin for Me

If you're tracking an employment-based or family-based green card priority date, you've probably spent years refreshing the Department of State’s Visa Bulletin every month between the 10th and 15th just to see if your cutoff date shifted.

It’s a tedious manual routine: navigate to the State Department site, locate the latest month's release, find your preference category and country column in two separate tables (Final Action Dates and Dates for Filing), and compare the dates against your priority date.

Instead of remembering to check manually, I set up a scheduled ChatGPT automation to monitor the official bulletin and notify me only when there's an actual update.

The Prompt Recipe

You can configure a scheduled task with a prompt structured like this:

Check the official U.S. Department of State Visa Bulletin page regularly.

Monitor the following parameters for my case:
- Category: [e.g., EB-2 / EB-3]
- Country of Chargeability: [e.g., India / China / All Other Areas]
- Priority Date: [e.g., January 15, 2023]
- Tables to watch: Both "Final Action Dates" and "Dates for Filing"

Only notify me if:
1. A new monthly bulletin has been published.
2. The cutoff dates for my specific category/country advance or retrogress.
3. My priority date becomes current under either table.

When reporting a change, provide:
- The previous cutoff date vs. the new cutoff date
- Whether the date is current
- A direct link to the official State Department bulletin source.

How It Works

  1. Official Source Anchoring: Explicitly instructing it to use the official Department of State Visa Bulletin page prevents it from summarizing second-hand immigration blogs or outdated forum posts.
  2. Delta-Only Alerts: Defining strict notification criteria prevents noise. If nothing has moved, it stays quiet until the next release.
  3. Structured Diffs: Asking for previous date, new date, and the official source link makes verifying the notification take under ten seconds.

A Quick Caveat: Use this strictly as an alerting mechanism, never as legal counsel. Whenever an alert fires, always cross-reference against the official travel.state.gov bulletin and the USCIS adjustment of status filing charts before taking any action.

It took two minutes to configure, and it completely eliminates the monthly chore of checking a static government webpage.
